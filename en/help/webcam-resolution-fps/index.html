Webcam resolution and FPS: what the numbers mean

Resolution describes the dimensions of a video frame; frame rate describes frames per second. Neither number alone tells you how a call looks. Separate the camera's advertised capabilities, the settings selected by an app and the video another participant actually receives.

On this page

  1. Read resolution as frame dimensions
  2. Distinguish configured FPS from measured delivery
  3. Compare settings with the same scene
  4. Choose settings for the problem you observe

Read resolution as frame dimensions

A setting such as 1280 × 720 describes a frame's width and height in pixels. The size of the preview on your screen is a separate quantity: a browser can display that frame in a smaller or larger box.

The camera test in this guide shows the dimensions reported for the current video track when the browser provides them. This is not a measurement of lens detail or a guarantee that a meeting app will select the same dimensions.

Distinguish configured FPS from measured delivery

The browser's getSettings() values describe the active track configuration. A reported frameRate is not a count of frames that successfully reached another person during a call.

A preview may still pause or show uneven motion. Do not present a configured value as a measured performance result, and do not infer a camera's maximum capability from a test using default settings.

Compare settings with the same scene

  1. Use your normal camera position and lighting. Note the original resolution and frame-rate settings if the app exposes them.
  2. Look at a stationary object, then move your hand across the frame at a steady pace.
  3. Change only one available setting and repeat both observations. Compare readable detail and motion separately.
  4. Finish with a short check in the app used for calls. Ask the other participant whether they see blur, blocks or pauses.

Choose settings for the problem you observe

More pixels will not correct a covered lens or establish why a browser cannot open the camera. A number that looks better in a settings menu is not automatically the better result for your setup.

Keep a change when the relevant picture or motion improves consistently. If the local preview is stable but the remote result is poor, collect the calling app's available connection diagnostics instead of continuing to change camera settings blindly.
