How to add a frame around your webcam in OBS

A webcam frame is usually a separate image placed above the camera source. The opening must be transparent for the video beneath it to remain visible. First get the camera working in the scene, then add and align the decoration.

On this page

  1. Check the image before importing it
  2. Add the frame above the camera
  3. Align the opening without stretching faces
  4. Troubleshoot the layers one at a time

Check the image before importing it

Use a frame you made or have permission to use. A PNG can contain transparency, but the extension alone does not guarantee it. Open the image in an editor that shows the alpha channel and inspect the center opening.

A checkerboard drawn into an image is not transparency. If that pattern appears as pixels inside the frame, it will cover the camera just like a solid color. Keep the original file so you can correct the opening without rebuilding the scene.

Add the frame above the camera

  1. In the same OBS scene as the working camera, add an Image source and select the frame file.
  2. Place the image source above the camera in the Sources list so its border is drawn on top.
  3. Position and resize the frame, then adjust the camera beneath it to fit the transparent opening.
  4. Use Edit Transform for precise positioning. Lock the arranged sources to reduce accidental movement while working on the scene.

Align the opening without stretching faces

Compare the opening's shape with the camera picture. If their proportions differ, resizing alone may leave gaps or distortion. Choose an appropriate frame or crop a deliberate part of the camera image while preserving the subject's proportions.

Move normally in front of the camera and check the border around the head and hands. A layout that fits a still face may clip gestures. Inspect the scene at its intended display size; an elaborate border can overwhelm a small camera window.

Troubleshoot the layers one at a time

If the whole picture disappears, hide the frame temporarily. A returning camera picture points toward image opacity, the opening or frame placement. If only the border is missing, check its visibility and its order relative to the camera.

Keep the image file in a stable local folder, since the Image source refers to that path. After moving assets, reopen the scene and check for missing images before relying on the saved layout.
