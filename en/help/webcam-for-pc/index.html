Connect a webcam to your PC

When a new webcam arrives, first establish that the computer recognizes it and that the intended app selects it. This sequence also helps when a camera stops working after moving a laptop to a different desk.

On this page

  1. Match the connection to your equipment
  2. Establish a direct connection
  3. Choose the camera in each app
  4. Investigate a missing device

Match the connection to your equipment

Read the model's instructions for supported operating systems and connections. A connector that fits is only the beginning: adapters, docks and extension cables become additional parts to check if the camera is absent.

Keep the model name handy. A support article for a similar-looking camera can describe controls or software that your device does not have.

Establish a direct connection

For the first check, connect the webcam directly to the computer where possible. Open a basic camera preview before adding a dock or other accessories. This creates a known starting point.

  1. Open the camera's physical privacy cover, if fitted.
  2. Connect the camera and wait for the system to recognize it.
  3. Check the device list and preview in the computer's camera application.

Choose the camera in each app

A laptop may expose its built-in camera alongside the USB webcam. Look for the intended model or device name in the app's video settings, then move a hand in front of that lens to confirm the choice.

Camera and microphone selections are separate. If a headset supplies your audio, selecting the webcam for video does not establish that the headset is the active microphone. Check both before a call.

Investigate a missing device

Try a second suitable USB port and repeat the same preview check. If the camera works directly but disappears through a dock, that comparison identifies the connection path to investigate next.

Use Windows Update and the manufacturer's model-specific support page for driver guidance. Avoid installing a bundle solely because its filename contains “webcam.” If another computer is available, a comparison there can help narrow the fault; it does not by itself prove a hardware diagnosis.
