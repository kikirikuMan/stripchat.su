Test your microphone and interpret the input level

A moving level meter shows that the selected input is receiving sound. To make that result useful, compare normal speech with silence and confirm which microphone is responding before adjusting its volume.

Local device test

Start only the device you want to check. The camera preview and microphone level stay in this browser; no recording is created. The test stops when this tab is hidden.

Prepare a repeatable sound check

Stop music or video playback near the microphone. Sit at the distance you expect to use during a call and check any physical mute switch on the headset or microphone.

  1. Press Test microphone and allow the requested microphone access.
  2. Say a short sentence at normal speaking volume.
  3. Pause for a few seconds and compare the meter during speech and silence.

Understand what the meter can tell you

A clear change during speech is evidence of an input signal. The meter does not play your voice back, score intelligibility or measure the sound received by another person. This tool does not make a recording. The level is a relative activity display, not a calibrated decibel or sound-pressure measurement.

If the meter moves while you are silent, listen for a fan, loudspeaker or other nearby sound. If it barely changes when you speak, verify the selected input before increasing every available volume control.

Select the intended microphone

A laptop, webcam and headset may each contribute an input. In Windows, open Settings, System, Sound and inspect Input. On Mac, use System Settings, Sound, Input.

Choose the intended device and repeat the same sentence. For example, moving a headset microphone closer should affect the signal from that headset; a laptop microphone across the desk will behave differently. Avoid tapping directly on a delicate microphone capsule.

Investigate silence or excessive level

Check the site's microphone permission and the operating system's access for your browser. A camera preview can work while microphone access remains blocked.

Adjust input volume in small steps while repeating normal speech. A meter pinned near its maximum leaves little information about quieter and louder parts; a listening test in a recording or calling app is needed to assess audible distortion.

Check the actual calling app

Stop this test, select the same microphone in the meeting app, and use that app's audio check. Its noise processing and device settings can produce a different result.

If the browser meter works but the meeting app receives nothing, inspect that app's mute state and input selection first. Preserve the comparison result so you do not restart hardware troubleshooting unnecessarily.
